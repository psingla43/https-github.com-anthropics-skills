Uncontested & Amicable Divorce · Ontario

When you and your spouse agree, the divorce should be the straightforward part.

If you have settled the questions that matter — parenting, support, and property — an uncontested divorce is largely a matter of preparing the right documents and filing them correctly. This page explains how that process works in Ontario and how our office can help you complete it.

Is this the right fit

An uncontested divorce is for spouses who are past the disagreement.

Not every separation needs to be fought. If the difficult conversations are behind you, the legal step of ending the marriage can be handled calmly and on paper. This route usually fits when:

  • You and your spouse agree on parenting arrangements for any children, or there are no children of the marriage.
  • You have resolved child support and, where it applies, spousal support — or agree that none is owed.
  • Property and debts have been divided, or a separation agreement already sets out how.
  • Neither spouse intends to ask the court to decide a disputed issue.
  • You can locate your marriage certificate, or can obtain one.
  • You are able to confirm the date you and your spouse began living separate and apart.

If some of these are not yet settled, the divorce may still be uncontested once they are. Often the more useful first step is a separation agreement that records what you have agreed. We can tell you which category your matter falls into during an initial consultation.

What the work involves

What we prepare, and what stays with the court.

An uncontested divorce is procedural, but the procedure has to be exact — a small error on the paperwork is the most common reason a filing is returned. Our role is to get the documents right the first time.

01

The divorce application

We prepare a simple or joint divorce application, confirm the grounds, and make sure the details match your marriage certificate and separation date.

02

Supporting documents

Marriage certificate, any required affidavit, and — where there are children — the information the court needs on support and arrangements.

03

Filing and service

We file with the appropriate court and, in a simple application, arrange for your spouse to be served and for proof of service to be filed.

04

Following the file through

We respond to any requisition from the court, correct anything sent back, and monitor the file until the divorce is granted.

05

Separation agreement, if needed

Where terms still need to be recorded, we can prepare or review a separation agreement so the divorce itself stays uncontested.

06

Your final documents

Once granted, we obtain the Divorce Order and, when it takes effect, the Certificate of Divorce you may later need.

The process

Five steps, from first call to final order.

The sequence below is typical. How long each step takes depends in part on the court, which is outside any lawyer's control.

  1. Initial consultation

    We talk through your situation, confirm the divorce is uncontested, and identify anything that still needs to be settled. You will leave knowing what the process looks like and what it will cost.

  2. Gathering documents

    You provide your marriage certificate, separation date, and details about any children. If a certificate needs to be ordered, we tell you how.

  3. Preparing and filing the application

    We draft the application and supporting documents for your review, then file them with the court. In a joint application, both spouses sign; in a simple one, your spouse is served.

  4. Court review

    A judge reviews the file. Where there are children, the court must be satisfied that appropriate support arrangements are in place before the divorce is granted.

  5. Divorce Order and certificate

    The court issues the Divorce Order. It becomes final 31 days later, and a Certificate of Divorce can then be obtained — the document you may need to remarry or update records.

Costs & timelines

Honest expectations, set out plainly.

We would rather be clear about cost and timing at the start than have either come as a surprise. The details below are general; we confirm specifics for your matter in the consultation.

Legal fees

For a straightforward uncontested divorce we can usually quote a fixed fee once we understand the details. If a separation agreement or extra steps are involved, we explain the cost of those separately before you commit.

Court filing fees

The court charges its own fees to file a divorce, separate from legal fees. These are set by the government and change from time to time; we confirm the current amount for you.

The one-year period

The most common ground for divorce is living separate and apart for one year. You can prepare and file before the year is complete, but the divorce is generally not granted until it has passed.

How long it takes

Once filed, processing time depends on the court's workload and whether anything needs correcting. This part is outside our control; we keep the file moving and tell you where it stands.

The final 31 days

After the Divorce Order is granted, there is a 31-day waiting period before it takes effect. Only then can a Certificate of Divorce be issued.

Common questions

Questions we are asked most often.

General answers to help you decide whether an uncontested divorce fits your situation. For advice on your own matter, please speak with us directly.

What is the difference between a simple and a joint divorce application?

In a joint application, both spouses apply together and sign the documents — no one has to be served. In a simple application, one spouse applies and the other is served with the papers. Both can be uncontested; the right choice depends on how much you and your spouse are cooperating and on your circumstances.

Do we have to be separated for a year before we can divorce?

One year of living separate and apart is the most common ground, but it is not the only one. Divorce can also be sought on other grounds recognized by the Divorce Act. You can generally prepare and file before the year is up, though the divorce is usually not granted until the period has passed. We can confirm what applies to you.

Can we divorce if we still live in the same house?

Sometimes. "Living separate and apart" looks at whether the marriage has genuinely ended, not only at separate addresses. Couples do sometimes remain under one roof for a period while separated. Whether that counts depends on the facts, and it is worth discussing.

What if we have children together?

You can still have an uncontested divorce, but the court will want to see that appropriate arrangements for the children — including child support consistent with the guidelines — are in place. The divorce can be delayed if the court is not satisfied on that point. We help make sure the file addresses it properly.

Do we need a separation agreement to get divorced?

No — a separation agreement and a divorce are different things. A divorce ends the marriage; a separation agreement records how you have divided property, support, and parenting. Many couples want both, and having the agreement in place first is often what keeps the divorce uncontested.

Can one lawyer act for both of us?

Even when a couple agrees on everything, a lawyer cannot represent both spouses' interests at once, because those interests can differ. We can prepare the divorce documents and act for one spouse; the other should have the opportunity to obtain their own independent legal advice. We will explain how this works in your situation.
